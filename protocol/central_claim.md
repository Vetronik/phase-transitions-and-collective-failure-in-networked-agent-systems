# Frozen central claim

## Primary claim

In the specified stochastic networked-agent model, communication has two qualitatively different operational effects: it can improve individual and collective decisions when private information is sufficiently diverse, and it can amplify correlated collective failure when agents repeatedly reuse information that shares a common error source.

## Required qualifications

- The claim is about the specified mathematical model and tested graph families, information generators, horizons, and parameter ranges.
- Agreement is not treated as evidence of intelligence. Accuracy, majority performance, tail risk, and error covariance are separate outcomes.
- The present paper does not claim a thermodynamic phase transition unless equilibrium sampling, finite-size scaling, and order-of-limits tests resolve one.
- A finite-horizon boundary, metastability, or sharp crossover is an acceptable result and will be named explicitly.

## Primary confirmatory contrasts

1. Independent inputs: `q=0.55`, `rho_in=0`, communication anchors `J=0` versus `J=3`.
2. Correlated inputs: `q=0.55`, `rho_in=0.25`, the same communication anchors.
3. Secondary quality check: `q=0.65` at both correlation levels.

The primary effect sizes are paired changes in individual accuracy and severe-failure probability. The mechanism endpoint is the change in aggregate error covariance and effective population size.
